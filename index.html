      How to use Recall

      1. Load a Deck

      A deck is the thing you open from My Decks. It holds exactly two kinds of content: one notes document (freeform Markdown) and any number of cards (question / answer pairs). Everything you import becomes one or both of those.

      Open the menu (☰, top-left) to get started:

      • + New Deck — start a blank deck and add cards manually
      • Import — choose one file or a whole batch of them, paste Markdown, or fetch a link. Recall then shows what it found and asks whether it should become notes, cards, or both, and where it lands: separate decks (one per file), one new deck, or appended to the deck you already have open. Nothing is created until you confirm
      • My Decks — every deck you've studied, saved on this device and mirrored to the cloud

      A plain Markdown page with ordinary headings is imported as notes by default — it is a document, not a stack of flashcards. Pick Cards in the review step if you do want each heading turned into a card.

      Study Notes 📝 — learn first, card later

      Every deck has a freeform Notes document beside its cards. Use the Cards ⇄ Notes toggle at the top of the study area (or Study Notes in the toolbar).

      • Write notes first — full Markdown: headings, math, diagrams, code, pasted images
      • Highlight → + Make card — select any fact (text, images, math) in the rendered notes; the floating button shows how much is captured. Tap it to preview the selection as the card's answer and frame the question that should bring it to mind (Ctrl+Enter to add)
      • Code survives the trip — highlight part of a code block and the card keeps it as a code block, in the same language as the block you took it from. A block whose fence never named a language gets one worked out from the code itself, falling back to TEXT when it isn't recognisably any language — so every block ends up with a badge and a copy button. A dashed pill means the language was detected rather than declared
      • Link one note to another — type [[ while editing and every note in your library becomes searchable from where the cursor already is; pick one and a ◈ link drops in. Links stay pointed at the right note even if you rename it, and a ↗ marks anything that leaves for the web, so you always know where a tap will take you
      • Link to a section, not just a note — add # after the note's name ([[Calculus#) and the list switches to that note's headings; pick one and the link opens the note scrolled straight to it. Typing it by hand works too: [[Calculus#The chain rule]] jumps to that heading, and [[#The chain rule]] jumps to one in the note you're already in
      • Name a note that doesn't exist yet — type any name after [[ and take Create…. You choose which folder it goes in, and you stay exactly where you were writing. Clicking a greyed-out link offers the same thing
      • Split a note that got too long — select the part that has outgrown its home and tap ↪ in the selection strip. It moves into a note of its own and leaves a link in its place
      • See what points here — open the ☰ On this page drawer in the notes header; under the headings, Linked from lists every note that references the one you're reading
      • Notes save, sync, and export together with the deck — or skip notes entirely and make cards directly, as before
      • On a phone, the header gets out of your way — scroll down and the deck title bar and Cards/Notes toggle fold away; scroll back up and they return. Tap ⤢ in the notes header to keep them hidden for good, and ⤡ to bring them back

      Back — your phone's back gesture (and the browser's back button) works the way you'd expect: it closes whatever is open, one layer at a time, then walks back through the notes and decks you've been through, returning you to the paragraph you were reading. Only once there's nothing left does it leave Recall, and it asks first.

      5. Edit & Format Cards

      Click the pencil icon on the question or answer face — or long-press the card with a mouse — to edit. Click the save icon to save. Edits are always committed when you navigate away. On phones and tablets, long-press selects text — use the pencil icon to edit.

      Select some text — reading or editing, it makes no difference — and the formatting bar appears with it. There is no permanent toolbar any more: every one of these buttons needs a selection to act on, so they now show up only when you have made one, and go away with it. On a phone the bar is a single row; tap ⋯ to fold out the styling controls.

      B I U SBold, Italic, Underline, Strikethrough
      </>Inline code
      […]Cloze — hide the selection as a fill-in-the-blank. In the editor it wraps the selection in {{ }}; in the rendered card or notes, select some text and tap the […] button to cloze it in place (tap again on the same text to un-cloze). Tap a cloze on the card to reveal it, or use the Reveal clozes button to flip them all at once (tap again to hide them; you can still tap any single one). In the notes header all three cloze tools sit together as one group, each drawn as what it does: a line of text with a solid blank in it and a + makes a cloze from your selection; an eye shows every answer at once (it gains a slash while they are showing, so tapping hides them again); and three stacked rows open Cloze Review — a list of every blank in the deck
      B I UFormat-in-place — rewrites the markdown under the selected text, in the rendered view and in the raw editor alike. These live on the floating bar that appears with your selection, alongside everything that captures one (cloze, + make a card, 📌 pin, highlight, ↪ split out, ⌫ delete). The notes header keeps the three cloze tools, which are about the note as a whole rather than about a selection

      Images 🖼️

      Add images three ways while editing a card:

      • Paste — copy any image (a screenshot, or an image from a webpage) and press Ctrl+V in the editor
      • Drag & drop — drag an image file onto the editor (desktop)
      • 🖼️ in the editor — tap the image button (it sits beside - and Tx in raw-edit mode, the three controls that act on the caret or the line rather than on a selection) to open your file picker. On phones and tablets this offers your camera or photo library

      Each image is optimized in your browser (downscaled to 1600px max and re-encoded to WebP) before being uploaded to your own Supabase Storage bucket; an ![](…) link is then inserted automatically. No separate key to set up — the same sign-in that unlocks sync unlocks image uploads. Images need a connection to upload. Hover a rendered image and use its 🗑 button to remove it from the note — since it's your own storage, that also deletes the file for good, unlike the old ImgBB host.

      Google Drive links just work: paste a public share link like ![](https://drive.google.com/file/d/FILE_ID/view) and it renders directly — no need to convert it first.

      6. Quick Notes 📌

      Found something worth keeping while reviewing an answer? Capture it without leaving your deck:

      • Open the answer in edit mode (pencil icon — long-press works on desktop — then flip to the answer side)
      • Select the text you want to keep
      • Click the 📌 button on the floating bar that appears with your selection

      The selection is saved as a new card in a dedicated quick_notes cloud deck (created automatically the first time). The selected text becomes the card’s question, with a blank answer for you to fill in later. A ✓ Saved to quick_notes toast confirms it worked. Open your quick_notes deck any time from My Decks. (Requires sign-in, since notes are stored in the cloud.)

      7. Track Progress

      The coloured bar at the top of the card area shows your session progress:

      Known Review Progress

      The header shows a live Known X / Review Y count, and each card carries a status badge. Replay filtered sessions (Review only, Known only, Uncategorized) from the buttons below the card.

      8. All Cards Panel

      Click to see every card in a scrollable Cornell-note view. You can edit, delete, or reorder cards from here.

      9. Sync & Cloud

      • Sync is automatic — every deck is mirrored to your account in the background; Sync Now forces an immediate two-way reconcile (newest copy of each deck wins)
      • My Decks — every deck, on this device and in the cloud, with a live sync-status column. Per deck: Load, Export (PDF / Markdown / JSON / SQL), Rename, Delete, and an inline category editor. Filter by category, select several decks for bulk Load / Categorize / Export / Delete, or Export All to back up the whole library. Deletes propagate to your other devices
      • Export Cards… (menu) — the current deck as Cornell PDF, Markdown, JSON, or SQL

      Every cloud action gives you instant feedback: a toast slides in at the top of the screen confirming it worked — for example ✓ Deck deleted everywhere or ✕ Sync failed — so you never have to wonder whether your sync, load, or delete went through. Click a toast to dismiss it early.

      10. Offline & My Decks 💾

      Once you’ve opened the app online once, it keeps working with no connection:

      • You stay signed in offline (after signing in online at least once), so you go straight to your decks.
      • Saving is automatic — every edit is written to the on-device library in the background; there is no manual save button. A reload starts on the clean home screen; reopen your deck from My Decks.
      • Every cloud deck is mirrored onto the device automatically, so the whole library is available offline — load, rename, or delete with no connection.
      • An Offline badge appears bottom-left when you lose connection.

      A first-time login needs internet. Edits made offline (including deletes) sync automatically once the connection returns.

      11. Card Format

      When importing or pasting Markdown, use the :: separator format:

      ::
What is the capital of France?
---
Paris
::

::
What year was the Eiffel Tower built?
---
1889
::

      Recall also reads <details> toggles, > blockquote toggles, Q:/A: pairs, and any ##–###### heading that ends in a question mark. All of these are unambiguous card syntax, so a file containing them is offered as Cards straight away.

      Plain ##/###/#### headings can be turned into card fronts too, with the text below as the back — but because a normal document looks exactly the same, Recall imports those as notes unless you choose Cards in the import review step.

      12. Themes & Style

      Click to choose from 10 built-in themes and a density preset. Everything else — font sizes, spacing, corner radii — lives under Advanced. Reset profile puts it all back. Desktop and mobile save separate style profiles, and your theme syncs with them.
